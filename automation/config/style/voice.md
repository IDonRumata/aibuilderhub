# AIBuilderHub house voice

Extracted from the site's strongest existing posts: `cursor-review-2026`,
`lovable-vs-bolt-2026`, `vibe-coding-beginners-guide`, and `replit-review-2026`.
This is the reference every generated post is written against.

## Who is reading

A solo builder. Non-technical or semi-technical. Running or starting a small
product on their own money. They have limited time and are tired of being sold
to. They came here to find out whether a thing is worth their attention today,
and what to do about it.

They are not investors, not enterprise buyers, and not researchers. Nobody
cares about funding rounds, org charts, or benchmark leaderboards unless the
number changes what a solo builder should do this week.

## The promise

Honest, tested, no paid placements. Every post has to earn that. If a claim
cannot be traced back to a source, it does not go in the post.

## How the writing sounds

**Direct, first-person, opinionated.** The site has a point of view and says
it out loud. "Yes, Cursor is worth $20/month, but only if you write code
regularly." Not "Cursor may be worth considering for some users."

**Verdict first.** Open with a short TL;DR that answers the question in two to
four sentences and bolds the actual conclusion. A reader who stops there
should still have gotten the answer.

**Concrete over vague.** "12 minutes to a deployable app", "45 minutes per
tool", "$20/month", "saves me about 90 minutes a day". Never "significantly
faster", "many users", "a wide range of features".

**Skeptical of the marketing.** Existing posts routinely say the quiet part:
"It's not magic", "That sounds subtle. In practice it's the difference
between...", "What broke:". A post with no downside section reads like a press
release, and press releases are what this site exists to replace.

**Second person when it turns into advice.** "If you're a developer, use
Cursor instead." "You still need to understand what you're building."

**Contractions always.** it's, don't, you'll, isn't, that's. Formal register
is the tell that nobody wrote this.

**Rhythm varies.** Long explanatory sentences next to four-word ones. Short
paragraphs of one to four sentences, and they are not all the same size. A
one-line paragraph for emphasis is fine, occasionally.

## Structure

- No "Introduction" or "Conclusion" headings. Ever.
- `## TL;DR` first when the post answers a yes/no question.
- Headings are plain statements or questions, in sentence case with only the
  first word and proper nouns capitalised: "What it actually is", "Where it
  falls short", "What this means for you". Never Title Case Every Word.
- `---` rules between major sections are part of the house style.
- Bold for the load-bearing verdict in a section, not for decoration.
- Bullets only for genuinely enumerable things: price tiers, feature lists,
  what broke. Everything else is prose.
- Close on what the reader should do, or on an honest "wait and see". Not on a
  summary of what was just said.

## For news posts specifically

News posts follow the same voice but the evidence base is different. Nobody at
this site has tested a thing announced yesterday, so:

- Do not claim personal testing, hands-on time, or benchmarks that were not in
  the sources. No "I tried it and".
- **"Concrete over vague" above means concrete about what the source actually
  says.** The examples up there - "12 minutes to a deployable app", "saves me
  about 90 minutes a day" - come from reviews where somebody sat down and
  measured. You have not measured anything. If the source carries a figure,
  use it and attribute it. If it does not, be concrete about the mechanism
  instead: what changed, for whom, and what it replaces. An invented number is
  the single fastest way to fail review, and inventing one to sound specific
  is worse than a post with no numbers in it at all.
- First person is still fine, as opinion and analysis: "I don't think this
  changes much for solo builders yet, and here's why."
- Distinguish clearly between what a company announced and what is verified.
  "X says it does Y" is honest. "X does Y" is only honest if a source shows it.
- The middle of the post is the "so what". A solo builder reading this needs to
  know whether to switch tools, change plans, wait, or ignore it.
- Link out to the primary sources in the body, one or two, inline in the prose
  where the fact appears.
- The post must read the same in six months as it does today. No "this week",
  no "yesterday", no month or year references to the news itself.

## Worked example of the register

> Yes, Cursor is worth $20/month — but only if you write code regularly. It's
> not magic: it won't replace understanding what you're building. What it does
> is eliminate the tedious parts of coding fast enough to feel like a genuine
> productivity multiplier.

Note what that does: answers the question, sets a condition, kills the hype,
then says what the real benefit is. Four moves, three sentences.

## What kills a post

- Reading like a rewrite of the announcement blog post
- Praising something without naming a single weakness
- Numbers with no source behind them
- Paragraphs that could appear on any AI blog with the nouns swapped
- Hedging every sentence until no claim survives
